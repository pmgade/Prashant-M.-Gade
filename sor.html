My Research Journey

Broad fields of my study have been statistical physics and nonlinear dynamics of spatially extended systems. I’m especially curious about how quenched disorder affects universality classes during phase transition. There is typically no discernible difference. Nonetheless, we have discovered instances in which a new universality class exists. Systems that exhibit power-law scaling across a range of parameters also pique my curiosity. I have experience in a variety of fields, and I choose and work on issues based on the institute’s requirements, students’ strengths and weaknesses, and the viability and significance of the problem. Here is a quick summary of my work. For more information, please refer to the source papers. Works from my Ph.D. program on wavelength doubling bifurcations, stability analysis of spatially periodic orbits, and long-term predictability of chaotic systems are omitted for the sake of conciseness. Not every post-PhD work is covered.

1. Synchronization:

In my opinion, my series of works in synchronization is my modest contribution to the overall theory of spatially extended nonlinear dynamical systems. I worked on synchronization in coupled dynamical systems with a focus on discrete- time, discrete-space system called coupled maps. The studies can be easily extended to coupled oscillators.
I studied random nonlocal couplings, Cayley tree, small world couplings, power- law coupling, couplings where sites are coupled to neighborhood which is a finite fraction of total sites, couplings with global feedback, local-global couplings and small world couplings (both quenched and annealed). In most cases, the criterion for synchronization is derived analytically. In general, we find that nonlocal couplings are necessary to achieve chaotic synchronization in coupled maps. In all the works mentioned above, we have also studied several problems apart from synchronization, namely, behavior of collective variables, nature of transition from synchronized to statistically independent behavior and control problems.

2. New Universality class of Dynamic Phase Transitions:

For a transition to synchronized 3-period state in coupled maps, we observed that the critical exponents do not match any known universality class. We modelled this behavior using a cellular automata model.
In coupled map lattice with simplicial coupling, we observe a new universality class in the transition to synchronization. There are very few known universality classes in the transition to the absorbing state and we believe that these are important findings.
In coupled map lattices with quenched directional disorder, we observed continuously varying power-laws at the critical point.
Replica synchronization in copies of coupled map lattices is known to be in the multiplicative noise universality class. With quenched disorder, the transition shows new superuniversal exponents independent of dimension. But it depends on the map and its parameters.

3. Power laws over a range of parameters:

In nature, power-laws are observed over a range of parameters while in physics they are observed only at the onset of continuous transitions. Self organized criticality (SOC) involving adiabatic drive and Griffiths phase involving quenched disorder are some of the proposed mechanisms. We worked on both.
a) We studied contact processes with topological disorder and processes with disorder in rules. We observed continuously varying power-laws over a range of parameters superposed with clean log-periodic oscillations. Such ”complex Griffiths phase” is not identified before.
b) We proposed a SOC model in coupled map lattice inspired by Bak-Sneppen model. System parameters are modified adiabatically. We observe power laws in various quantifiers. We proposed a SOC model inspired by an experiment on bird flights. Instead of usual models in which disturbance branches and spreads, we model processes in which disturbance diffuses, spreads and the system shows SOC.
c) We also observe power-laws over a range of parameters without quenched disorder or adiabatic drive. i)We find a power-law over a range of parameters in negatively coupled circle maps in the regime that shows travelling waves. ii)For 1d zigzag and 2-d checkerboard patterns in coupled map lattices, we defined appropriate quantifiers based on local slope. In 1d, the persistence exponent is 3/8 over a range of parameters. In 2d the persistence exponent is 0.22. These exponents match the Ising model with Glauber update at zero temperature. Surprisingly, in 2d, the dynamic exponent matches the 2d Ising model at a critical temperature which is nonzero.

4. Dynamic Phase Transitions to known universality classes:

We have been looking at parallels between phase transitions in stochastic systems and those in spatially extended deterministic dynamical systems. We introduced two new quantifiers, persistence and flip rate, in this context.
a) Derrida showed that one dimensional Ising model with Glauber update has persistence exponent 3/8 at zero temperature. We obtained the same exponent for i) coupled logistic maps ii) coupled logistic maps with even delay iii) coupled logistic maps on a ladder. This shows the intriguing connection between known equilibrium models with coupled logistic maps.
b)We showed that the synchronization transition in a system with local-global coupling is indeed in the DP class. Even transition to a frozen random pattern is shown to be in the DP class.
c) We studied the contact process under several rules. We observe that despite relaxing several prerequisites of the Janssen-Grassberger conjecture the transition is still in the DP universality class.
d)Maps with delay can be mapped on a spatiotemporal system and a transition from a state with coarse-grained periodic state to a fully aperiodic state can be viewed as a dynamic phase transition. We observe well-defined persistence exponents. For logistic map, the transition is in directed Ising class.
e) We studied the contact process on the random fractal. The critical exponents interpolate between one and two dimensions as expected. The persistence exponent is much larger because it takes a long time for the infection to reach some sites.
f) We showed that the persistence exponent in five known models in the directed Ising class are identical and close to unity.

5. Fractional order maps:

We carried out a series of studies on fractional order maps.
a) We defined fractional order maps of complex order and derived stability conditions for the fixed point for a linear system.
b) For maps with complex order or complex maps with real fractional order, we observed that chaos can be seen only for non-analytic functions.
c) We found stability conditions for the synchronization in the coupled fractional maps on a lattice.
d) We derived stability conditions for synchronization of coupled maps of incommensurate fractional order.
e) Unlike integer order systems, the stability conditions for the periodic state in fractional order maps are nontrivial. We were able to derive stability conditions for periodic orbit with period 2.
f) We studied delayed maps and use of delayed feedback to stabilize the fixed point. The stability criteria are derived analytically. In all these cases, the results are corroborated by numerical simulations.
g) We studied certain fractional order maps, such as neurons or r-deformed maps in further detail.

6. Stochastic Resonance (SR) and Spatial Stochastic Resonance :

We studied SR in maps and coupled maps. We defined maps which had two states (not necessarily fixed points) switching between which is aided by noise (additive or multiplicative). Apart from SR, we studied the kinetic aspects like hysteresis and array enhanced (or reduced) SR.
In the Ising model, we explored, analytically as well as numerically, the effect of temperature on response to spatially periodic fields using th transfer matrix method. We find that the for low enough magnetic field intensities, the correlation between external magnetic field and the response in magnetization shows a maximum for a given temperature. This novel ”spatial SR” has different origin from classical stochastic resonance phenomenon

7. Miscellaneous:

In 93-94, it was found that different physical systems driven by identical random noise behave identically asymptotically. We showed that it was due to the structural instability of the attractor.
We studied coupled neuronal models with small-world lattice with annealed con- nections and showed that even its phase diagram has a striking similarity with the local-global model.
An experimental work on a transition from a coarse-grained periodic pattern to spatiotemporal chaos in a system of coupled diode resonators showed glassy behavior. It was modelled using model of spatially extended system with disorder.
Unlike equilibrium systems, turning on the nonlocal connections does not immediately lead to dramatic changes in properties in dynamical systems. The transition to phase synchronization in epidemiological models was found to depend as much on the timescales of the dynamics involved as the topology of the underlying network.
We studied the random walk model in which the sites visited are marked periodically or with some probability. The walker avoids these marked sites. We find that the deterministic model is in the same universality class as self avoiding walk while the probabilistic model is in a new class.
We have studied asset exchange models and were able to reproduce empirical features of wealth distribution in societies. I also studied game-theoretic models in ecology. We proposed a generalized competitive exclusion principle for pattern formation. We showed that if the resource is scarce, the growth is fractal and while compact patterns merge, fractals may not.
I have also worked on physics education and proposed an experiment in symbolic dynamics in the computational lab in M.Sc.
